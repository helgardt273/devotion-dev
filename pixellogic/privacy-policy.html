Pixel Logic privacy policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

Pixel Logic is published by Devotion Dev.

Summary

Pixel Logic is an offline puzzle game. It has no account and no sign-up, and the game itself does not send your data anywhere. The app shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects some data as described below.

What the app stores on your device

  • Your settings (theme, mistake checking, lives mode, timer and highlighting).
  • Your progress: solved pictures (the album), best times, statistics, the daily streak and puzzles in progress.
  • The time until which ads are switched off if you watched a reward video.

This data stays on your device. Android's own backup may include it in your personal Google backup if you have that turned on. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app uses Google AdMob to show banner ads on menus and results screens, occasional full-screen ads between puzzles, and optional reward videos. AdMob may collect and process device identifiers (such as the advertising ID), IP address and approximate location derived from it, and information about ad interactions and app performance, to serve and measure ads and to prevent fraud. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Consent (Google User Messaging Platform)

Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA and the UK), the app asks for your consent using Google's User Messaging Platform before personalised ads are shown. You can change your choice at any time from Settings > Privacy & ad choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your device settings.

Children

Pixel Logic is meant for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13.

Other permissions and Google Play services

Google's ads SDK and Android libraries also add the advertising-ID, Android ad-services (Privacy Sandbox) and background-work permissions (such as wake lock and foreground service). The app does not show a prompt for these. The "Privacy & ad choices" button appears only where consent applies. The app may also show Google Play's in-app review prompt, which Google Play handles under its own terms.
