Tile Triples: privacy policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026

Summary

Tile Triples is an offline puzzle game published by Devotion Dev. It has no account and no sign-up. The game itself does not collect, send or sell any personal information. The app shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects some data as described below.

What is stored on your device

  • Your settings (theme, vibration, timer).
  • Your level progress, stars, daily puzzle streak and statistics.
  • The game in progress (auto-saved so you can continue later).
  • When an ad-free period you earned by watching a video ends.

This data stays on your device. It may be included in your own Android device backup to your Google account if you have backups switched on. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

The app shows banner ads on menu and result screens, occasional full-screen ads between levels, and optional rewarded videos that you choose to watch. Ads are provided by Google AdMob. Google may collect and use your device's advertising ID, approximate location derived from your IP address, information about ad interactions and diagnostic data, to show ads, measure them and prevent fraud. See how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Consent

Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA and the UK), the app asks for your consent with Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) before personalised ads are shown. You can change your choice at any time in Settings > Privacy & ad choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings.

Permissions

The app itself declares only internet and network-state access, which are needed to load ads. It does not access your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files.

Children

Tile Triples is meant for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date.

Other permissions and Google Play services

Google's ads SDK and Android libraries also add the advertising-ID, Android ad-services (Privacy Sandbox) and background-work permissions (such as wake lock and foreground service). The app does not show a prompt for these. The "Privacy & ad choices" button appears only where consent applies. The app may also show Google Play's in-app review prompt, which Google Play handles under its own terms.
